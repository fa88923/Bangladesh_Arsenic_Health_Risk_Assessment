# Limitations Register (Phases 6–9)

Owner: Member 4. Every limitation that must appear in the final report (master plan section 15). Each entry gives the reason, the size of its effect where measured, and where the evidence lives. Member 5 carries these into Phase 10.

Status key: **open** = must be stated in the final report; **quantified** = open, and its effect has been measured; **mitigated** = reduced by a decision, but still to be stated.

## Phase 8 (convergence)

| ID | Limitation | Why it exists | Effect / evidence | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L19 | **Bogra mean HI does not converge** within N ≤ 20,000 | The Lognormal with σ_log = 2.86 gives single draws a CV of about 60; a 5% CV on the mean needs about 1.4 M iterations | Between-seed CV is 26–27% at 10k and 15–22% at 20k. Report Bogra's median and P95; give its mean only with a caveat | quantified | convergence D19 |
| L20 | **P95 HI and P95 ELCR** need N = 20,000 in 4 of 20 cells (Bogra adult and child, Chittagong child, Sylhet adult) | The upper tail stabilises more slowly than central statistics | The r0 value at 10k differs from 20k by up to 9.1% (Bogra child). Between-seed CV at 10k is up to 8.6%. The primary N = 10,000 is kept for comparability with the paper | quantified | convergence D18 |
| L21 | **P50 HI** is unstable in 8 cells | The median lies in the extrapolated sub-detection-limit region (see L3) | CV up to 17% (Rajshahi adult, median HI 0.0002). Only Dhaka child (0.71) and Barisal child (1.98) are near decision-relevant values | quantified | convergence D19 |
| L22 | The convergence criterion (5% / 1 point) is a **project choice** | The plans specify the procedure but not a numeric tolerance | A stricter tolerance would flag more cells; all per-cell values are published so readers can apply their own | open | convergence D16 |
| L23 | Only **5 seeds** per N | Project choice (design doc section 17.2); the number is not a universal standard | The between-seed SD is itself estimated with about ±35% relative error | open | convergence report |
| L24 | Monte Carlo error is **not** the full uncertainty | Parameter uncertainty in the fitted C and BW distributions does not shrink with N | **Quantified in Phase 9:** the P95 HI 95% interval width is 0.45–3.5× the primary value, against an MC CV of 2–9% | quantified | convergence report |

## Phase 9 (sensitivity and uncertainty)

| ID | Limitation | Why it exists | Effect / evidence | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L25 | **Fitted-parameter uncertainty is large** | 44–110 wells per district, many censored | P95 HI 95% intervals run, for example, from 1.6 to 24.8 for Chittagong adults (primary 6.7) and from 0.8 to 13.8 for Bogra adults (primary 6.6). Comilla is the narrowest (40.6–65.1, primary 51.3). Report the intervals next to the primary values | quantified | sensitivity report, section 2 |
| L26 | **Family choice** changes P95 and the mean far more than P(HI>1) | The tails are extrapolated beyond the measured wells | P95 changes by up to ×3.1 and the mean by up to ×46, but P(HI>1) by at most ×1.19 (S1–S3). Use P(HI>1) as the headline metric | quantified | sensitivity D27 |
| L27 | IR, EF, ET and SA parameters are **held fixed** in the two-dimensional analysis | Base-paper assumptions with no data; no sampling uncertainty can be estimated | The parameter-uncertainty intervals cover C and BW only; the IR/SA *interpretation* is tested separately (S6: ×3.2–3.5 on P95) | open | sensitivity D26 |
| L28 | The ± reading (L11) is the **largest single modelling choice** | See Phase 6 D1 | The log-space reading gives ×3.2–3.5 on P95 HI and up to ×2.35 on P(HI>1). The arithmetic reading is physically required, but team sign-off is still pending | quantified | sensitivity S6 |
| L29 | The regenerated arsenic bootstrap matches Phase 4 to **1.1×10⁻⁶**, not bit for bit | Member 2 ran under numpy 2.5.3 / scipy 1.18.1; `requirements.txt` pins numpy 1.26.4 / scipy 1.17.1 | Negligible numerically, but it is a **reproducibility gap**: `requirements.txt` does not reproduce the Phase 4 environment exactly. | L30 | Rao–Wu bootstrap approximations | Standard (n_h − 1) with-replacement scheme; no finite-population correction; the arsenic and BW bootstraps are paired arbitrarily | Appropriate for many PSUs per stratum (≥ 6 here); the effect is negligible because BW uncertainty is small | open | sensitivity D24 |
